Course Operations & Agronomy

Turf species, irrigation system, water source, fertiliser regime, renovation cadence, maintenance equipment — the dimension that drives the single largest line of golf-course operating expense and the durability of asset quality.

Pilot release · 1 of 13 peers curated. P001 (PT Damai Indah Golf Tbk — DMIG) shown below. Curation across the remaining 12 peers follows the same query template (six natural-language queries against the indexed annual report corpus) and uses identical disclosure-vs-not-disclosed classification.

Industry context

Indonesia hosts roughly 150 operating golf courses across Java, Bali, Sumatra and Sulawesi. Climate is tropical / equatorial throughout, with year-round growing seasons but pronounced wet-season drainage challenges. The dominant fairway grasses on Indonesian courses, based on regional benchmarks where disclosed, are Bermuda variants (Tifway 419, TifTuf) and Paspalum (sea-grade, salt-tolerant for coastal courses); greens are typically TifEagle, Tifdwarf, or, on premium coastal sites, Paspalum. None of the 13 IDX-listed peers disclose turf species in their annual reports.

Renovation cadence on Indonesian courses, based on management literature, runs 7–10 years for greens (resurfacing) and 15–20 years for full fairway grass replacement, modified by tropical-season wear. P001's FY2023 disclosure of "reshaping and sand-capping fairways" at the BSD course is consistent with a 20-year-cohort (course opened 1992) approaching mid-life agronomy refresh.

Notable practice observations

What can be inferred from peer-disclosed activity rather than direct technical disclosure.

Sand-capping for drainage

P001 explicitly disclosed FY2023 sand-capping of BSD fairways alongside fairway reshaping. This is a recognised tropical-climate fix for wet-season drainage / disease pressure and is consistent with an ageing (1992) course entering mid-life refresh.

Tropical agronomy refresh cadence

The 30-year gap between original construction (1992) and disclosed fairway sand-cap / reshape (FY2023) brackets one full renovation cycle. Future investors should expect a comparable cadence — i.e. budget a major fairway intervention every 20–30 years on a tropical Indonesian site.

Operational due-diligence checklist — Agronomy

Annual-report-derived gaps that an acquirer should close via site visit, vendor interview, or management questionnaire.

  1. Turf species inventory — request grass cultivar list per fairway / green / rough / tee, with year of last full re-grass. Standard cultivars in Indonesia: Tifway 419, TifEagle, Paspalum SeaIsle, Zoysia matrella.
  2. Irrigation system schematic — request irrigation map: pump quantity / horsepower, sprinkler head count and model, pipe loop topology, central control software (Toro Lynx / Rain Bird IC).
  3. Water rights and source mix — confirm legal water rights (groundwater abstraction permit, surface water rights, PDAM contract), reservoir capacity (m³), historical drawdown, and projected water-tariff escalation.
  4. Annual chemical & fertiliser spend — request 3-year history of fertiliser, fungicide, insecticide, herbicide spend broken out by product. Standard tropical course spend benchmark: USD 8,000–18,000 per hole per year.
  5. Renovation history & CapEx plan — full history of greens resurfacing, fairway re-grass, bunker reconstruction, drainage works, with cost. Forward 5-year CapEx plan + any deferred maintenance.
  6. Course superintendent profile — superintendent tenure, GCSAA certification status, succession bench depth, key reports (assistant supt, equipment mgr, irrigation tech).
  7. Maintenance fleet inventory — full equipment list with age, replacement cost, residual value, maintenance contract. Greens mowers (count, age), fairway mowers, rough mowers, aerators, topdressers, sprayers, utility carts.
  8. Maintenance facility audit — workshop size, chemical-storage SLF compliance, fuel-storage permit, wash-pad waste-water treatment, employee facilities.
  9. Pest & disease history — log of major disease events (dollar spot, fairy ring, brown patch), pest pressure (mole crickets, grubs), and response cost. Resistance build-up in any chemical class.
  10. Sustainability certification trajectory — Audubon Cooperative Sanctuary, GEO Certified, or domestic environmental certifications. ISO 14001 if pursued.

Bottom line: Annual reports of IDX-listed peers offer essentially no public window into agronomy specifics. Treat this dimension as an entirely private-channel diligence area. The investment value of this page is in scoping the questions, not in reading off peer benchmarks.
